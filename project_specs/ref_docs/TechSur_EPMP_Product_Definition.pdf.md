01   /   PRODUCT DEFINITION
Executive product direction

A focused product definition extracted from TechSur’s 12-page emergency-management market and technology
research summary.

PRODUCT THESIS
Develop an open, AI-enabled Emergency Preparedness & Management Platform (EPMP) that supports
preparedness through recovery, offers a shared operating picture, and interoperates with incumbent government
systems.

Three strategic anchors

ANCHOR PRODUCT INTENT

01 Continuous preparedness Keep plans, capabilities, people, facilities, and resources ready before a crisis.

02 Coordinated emergency operations Connect incidents, command roles, resources, tasks, decisions, communications, and
GIS in one operational view.

03 Intelligent improvement Convert exercises, incidents, observations, and outcomes into evidence-based
readiness improvements.

Commercial stance

Operate in two modes: a complete emergency-management workspace for organizations without an established
platform, and an interoperability / readiness layer for agencies retaining WebEOC, Veoci, Everbridge, and other
investments. This is a design recommendation inferred from the attached research.

Evidence boundaries

The attached document compares six direct competitors and five adjacent tools; many competitive observations are
vendor-reported or remain unverified. TechSur’s FEMA NFIP PIVOT experience concerns mission-system database
administration and operations. Its FDA EMS material is an awarded solution approach, not evidence of a completed
implementation.

Source basis: Executive Summary.pdf, pp. 1–2, 6–7, 11–12. This brief does not independently re-verify vendor claims or government procurements.

Business context & use cases

Emergency response typically requires coordination across applications, agencies, command structures, facilities, and
jurisdictions.

BUSINESS CHALLENGE OPERATIONAL IMPACT PRODUCT RESPONSE

Scattered plans & risk records Readiness is difficult to substantiate. Versioned plans, risk assessments, exercises,
evidence.

Disconnected event feeds Slow incident validation and incomplete

Multiple communication

 Delays and uncertain message

Reports assembled after the

Representative business scenarios

• State or regional EOC: Monitor an approaching hazard, activate incident roles, share information with local partners, and
prioritize resources.

• County or municipal response: Collect field observations, manage assignments, distribute alerts, and compile an auditable
event timeline.

• Federal mission continuity: Track affected facilities, operational dependencies, staff readiness, and restoration activities
during a disruption.

PRODUCT BOUNDARY
The EPMP should coordinate and integrate specialized alerting, GIS, CAD, and infrastructure systems rather than
assume responsibility for every external system of record.

09   /   PRODUCT DEFINITION
Interoperability standards & guardrails

STANDARD / PATTERN APPLICATION WITHIN EPMP

OpenAPI + REST / JSON Versioned service contracts for incident, resource, readiness, reporting and
administration functions.

OAuth 2.0 / OIDC / SAML 2.0 Delegated API authorization and federation with an agency’s identity provider.

Webhooks / event bus Publish incident and status changes, with durable delivery, idempotency and retry
policies.

OASIS Common Alerting Protocol (CAP) Emergency alert representation and exchange; approved external warning
integration.

OASIS EDXL, including RM / DE Emergency information distribution and applicable resource messaging with
partners.

NIEM data models Cross-agency semantic exchange and standardized event / organization concepts.

OGC services / GeoJSON / Esri REST Consistent geospatial feature exchange, layers and map interoperability.

STIX / TAXII (optional) Threat-information exchange for cyber-related continuity scenarios.

FHIR (optional) Healthcare-specific exchange only where a regulated medical use case requires it.

Four non-negotiable integration behaviors

• Record authority: Choose which system creates, updates and reconciles each incident, person, asset and alert record.

• Trusted provenance: Preserve source, version, timestamps, identity and confidence; do not turn unverified feeds into
operational facts.

• Controlled federation: Policy-enforce every cross-jurisdiction exchange and maintain revocable access.

• Graceful failure: Queue events, provide retry / reconciliation, and operate in degraded mode when interfaces are offline.

CLARIFICATION IN THE SOURCE
The report references CAP for alerts and also loosely associates CAP with resource dispatch. The proposed
interface design keeps CAP for alerts and EDXL resource messaging / agency-approved request formats for
resource requests; detailed standards profiles must be validated.

Architecture decisions

• Modular domains: Plans / readiness, incidents / ICS, resources, alerts, continuity, AAR and administrative services.

• Customer control: Open data exports, documented schemas, portable containers and replaceable infrastructure services.

• Federated collaboration: Separate tenant data and identities while permitting explicitly authorized incident sharing.

13   /   PRODUCT DEFINITION
AI applications & release roadmap

AI is a governed assistance layer across preparedness, live operations, and after-action learning—not a replacement
for incident command.

PHASE AI USE CASE EXPECTED OPERATIONAL VALUE

Phase 1 Signal triage, clustering and anomaly screening on approved
risk feeds. Reduce noise and identify candidate events for
validation.

Phase 1 Draft structured incident information / assist completing ICS
forms from reviewed notes. Reduce transcription and manual data entry.

Phase 2 Conversational knowledge assistant grounded in EOPs, SOPs
and incident records. Faster retrieval with citations and access control.

Phase 2 Incident summaries, SITREP and notification draft generation. Accelerate reporting and communications
preparation.

Phase 2 Predictive hazard / impact models and resource requirement
forecasts. Support scenario comparison and capacity planning.

Phase 2 Recommendations for response role / resource matches. Assist resource coordinators, subject to
qualifications and review.

Phase 3 Specialized agent workflows for data gathering and draft AAR
/ communications. Automate limited administrative workflows with
explicit approvals.

Phase 3 Role-adaptive analytical dashboards and recurring lessons
identification. Turn response evidence into improving readiness.

AI capability architecture

Approved operational sources → permission-aware retrieval and structured data tools → model-agnostic AI service
→ validated draft / recommendation → human approval → auditable update through existing business APIs.

SAFEGUARD
No AI-generated message should be automatically issued as a public warning, evacuation instruction or binding
resource deployment decision. The attachment explicitly calls for human approvals, suggestion logs and safety
testing.

Source basis: Executive Summary.pdf, pp. 6, 8–9, 11.
